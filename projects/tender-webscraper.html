Tender Webscraper

My first taste of AI-assisted coding: using ChatGPT to turn a daily manual chore into an automated pipeline.

Period
Jan–Apr 2025
For
BD team, ST Logistics
AI partner
ChatGPT
Stack
Python, BeautifulSoup

The whatA shortlist waiting every morning

A Python webscraper built for the Business Development team at ST Logistics. It runs on a daily schedule, scans public tender portals for newly posted opportunities, filters them against relevance criteria, and compiles the results into a structured output ready for review.

Before this, someone on the team spent part of every morning loading the same pages, cross-referencing entries and copying data into a spreadsheet. The scraper reduced that to zero. The list is there when they arrive.

The whyTen minutes a day adds up

Repetitive data collection is an obvious automation target, but it's easy to leave alone because it only takes ten minutes. Over a year those minutes become days, and they land in the exact window when focus matters most.

The goal was reliability as much as speed: no missed tenders because of a busy morning, and no inconsistent filtering depending on who ran the check.

How it was builtLearning Python with a tutor on call

Without a formal programming background, I leaned on ChatGPT throughout: to guide Python syntax decisions, debug unexpected HTML structures in the tender portals, and work out how to parse pages that loaded content dynamically.

The loop was simple. I described what I wanted to extract, ChatGPT suggested an approach, I tried it, hit a wall, and went back with the error message. Over a few weeks that loop taught me more about requests, BeautifulSoup and exception handling than any tutorial. The AI shortened the distance between knowing what I wanted and knowing how to get there.

What I learnedThree lessons

  1. AI-assisted coding lowers the barrier, not the bar.ChatGPT got me past syntax hurdles fast, but understanding why the code worked was still on me. The scraper only became reliable once I stopped copy-pasting suggestions and started reading the documentation they pointed to.

  2. Scraper fragility is the real maintenance cost.HTML structures change without warning. I added lightweight structural checks that raise an alert instead of silently returning empty results, so a site change gets caught that morning instead of two weeks later.

  3. The output format matters as much as the data.A raw dump is as bad as no automation. I worked with the team to understand how they used the shortlist and shaped the output around their existing Excel workflow, so adoption was immediate.
