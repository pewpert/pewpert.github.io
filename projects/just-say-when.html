just say when.

The fastest way for a group to pick a time everyone can make. Solo-built, no framework, free to run.

The whatMark when you're free. See the overlap.

A group scheduling app. Anyone creates a group and shares a six-character invite code. Each person marks when they're free, either in Basic mode (morning, afternoon or evening, one tap per day) or Advanced mode (30-minute slots). The app shows the overlap as a heatmap and turns the banner green when everyone matches: "You all found a time."

If someone hasn't answered, the banner says so by name. That small nudge does a lot of the work of getting everyone to respond.

The why"When's good?" in the group chat

Picking a time with three or more people in a group chat takes hours. Someone proposes Thursday, someone can't, Saturday morning gets suggested, someone has plans until three, and the question gets asked again next week.

Doodle and When2Meet solve this but feel dated, are awkward on phones, and ask everyone to sign up. My goal was to remove every step that isn't marking your time: share a code, tap your slots, see the overlap.

How it was builtSix product decisions

Two modes, chosen per meetup. Basic for dinners and brunches, simple enough for older relatives. Advanced for offsites and work meetings. Same data underneath.

Guest sign-in next to Google. Invitees tap "Continue as guest", type a name and they're in, using Firebase Anonymous Auth. They can upgrade to a Google account later to keep their groups across devices. This removed the biggest source of drop-off.

Tap to toggle, drag to scroll. The first version used drag-to-paint on touch, and it quietly corrupted data: iOS Safari fires a synthetic mousedown about 300ms after touchend, which flipped cells back off. It took three iterations to fix. The shipped version arms a tap, checks a movement threshold, and ignores synthetic mouse events for 500ms.

Live updates. Firestore onSnapshot listeners mean everyone sees each other's availability appear without refreshing. A per-user membership index avoids collection-group queries and the composite indexes they need.

Rules enforced on the server. Firestore security rules lock every meetup field except the agenda to its creator, so the protection holds even if someone bypasses the UI. Deleting a group cleans up its leftover documents safely.

No build step. Plain ES modules loaded directly. It loads in under a second on cable and under three on 3G.

What I learnedFour lessons

  1. The bugs that matter are the silent ones.Three rounds of QA caught about 20 bugs. The worst was a date helper using toISOString().slice(0,10), which shifts every date back a day for anyone east of UTC. Every meetup in Singapore and Tokyo would have shown the wrong day, and nothing would have looked broken.

  2. Removing sign-up mattered more than any feature.Every scheduling tool loses people at "create an account". Letting guests in with just a name fixed that, and the upgrade path keeps the option open for people who want it.

  3. Test on real phones.The Safari tap bug and a scroll-position jump during live updates never showed up in desktop emulation. Mobile browsers have their own ideas about touch and scroll, and only a real device shows them.

  4. No framework means you own every line.I wrote my own state handling and event delegation. The app is small and fast and I understand all of it, which made debugging quicker. The trade-off is that there was no one else's abstraction to lean on when something broke.
